# Preview is an explicit camera state

Every camera has an explicit preview state. `start_preview(region=..., max_size=...)` enters it, or reconfigures it when the camera is already previewing, and `stop_preview()` and `close()` leave it. While previewing, `preview_frame(not_before=None)` pulls one blocking `PreviewFrame`: the pixels, their pixel format, a timestamp, and the `PreviewGeometry` they show. Outside preview, `preview_frame()` raises, and so do `capture_frame()` and `capture_files()` during preview. A consumer that wants a capture during preview takes a snapshot: stop, capture, start. Making the state explicit keeps the costs visible. A GenICam camera has to restart acquisition to change ROI or binning, and a gphoto2 body has to switch live view on and off, so preview can't be a stateless call that changes the camera behind the caller's back.

A preview keeps the camera's resolution and current pixel format unless the caller asks for less. The library does only what the camera alone can do: cut what crosses the link, and say exactly what each frame shows. Zooming, panning, scaling for display, histograms and focus metrics belong to the consumer, which can do them instantly on a full-resolution stream. The **preview region** is a box in fractions of the sensor, and `max_size` (default `None`, no reduction) caps the longest side of the output for consumers that want less data, such as thumbnails of several cameras. Each driver chooses its own mechanism and reports what it applied as a `PreviewGeometry`, which `camera.preview` also returns (`None` when not previewing). The geometry also gives the capture's size in pixels and the preview's scale against it, so a consumer can tell the user whether a view shows every pixel a capture would. A `min_size` floor can go with the cap: since cameras reduce in steps only the driver knows, a consumer asks for at least a view's pixels and gets the nearest step above it. Each `PreviewFrame` carries its format's `bit_depth` and the `full_scale` a saturated pixel reads, as captures do, so a consumer never has to guess where clipping is. GenICam uses hardware ROI and decimation where they work, and crops and strides on the host where they don't. gphoto2 uses `liveviewzoomarea` and `liveviewsize`, and reports them read-only because preview owns them. Fractions are used because gphoto2 can't express a live-view crop in sensor pixels, and a box in pixels would only fit GenICam. Preview shares every other setting with capture, including the trigger mode and continuous AF, and settings can change during preview. A camera that can't time its frames refuses `not_before`, as it does for captures. The canonical `width` and `height` keep describing captures.

## Considered Options

- **A stateless `preview_frame()` that switches geometry on demand**: the previous API. Alternating previews and captures would each pay a hidden restart, and gphoto2's live view was already latent state, started by the first preview and ended only by `close()`.
- **Capture during preview leaves and resumes preview automatically**: convenient, but it costs two restarts per capture on GenICam, and those restarts would be invisible to the caller.
- **The caller choosing binning, decimation or stride through native settings**: more control, but every consumer would need per-model knowledge to get a cheap preview, and the result couldn't be compared across cameras.
- **Display-ready previews (8-bit, capped at 1280 px by default)**: the first design. Spectral work runs at 10 bits and more, and exposure and focus are judged on real pixel values at full resolution, so the consumer needs control of both. Converting for display is cheap for the consumer and lossy for the library.
- **Frames pushed from a driver thread**: rejected by ADR 0001. Consumers already poll on their own timers.
- **A no-repeat rule instead of `not_before`**: repeats only add lag on a display. What matters for focus scoring is a frame taken after the lens moved, which `not_before` states directly, as it does for `capture_frame()`.

## Consequences

- This breaks the previous `preview_frame()`, which worked at any time.
- `docs/guide.md` and the camera contract suite cover the preview state for every driver and both simulated cameras.
